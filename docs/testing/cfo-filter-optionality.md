## Formulas and hand checks

1. **One-time spend.** Base monthly surplus = recurring income excluding bonuses and one-time income − planned costs including required debt minimums. Annual surplus = 12 × base monthly surplus. Remaining annual surplus = annual surplus − purchase. Monthly EF allocation = max(remaining annual surplus, 0) / 12 × method EF share; debt extra uses the method debt share. No $800 cap. EF months = ceil(EF gap / allocation), or unknown/unreachable with no allocation; a zero gap takes zero months. Spending beyond the annual surplus is explicitly described as drawing from savings.

   $1,000 monthly base surplus means $12,000 annual surplus. With a $12,000 EF gap, no purchase allocates $500 monthly and takes 24 months. A $500 purchase leaves $11,500; exact monthly allocation is `1,150,000 / 12 × 50/100 = 143750/3` cents ($479.1666…, displayed as $479.17 if rounded). `ceil(1,200,000 / (143750/3)) = ceil(576/23) = 26`. Rational cents avoid rounding before the month calculation. Talun: $5,400 income − $4,390 costs = $1,010 monthly; a $100 purchase leaves $12,020 annually.

2. **Flip the light.** These are monthly income lifts, not the household's existing surplus. For each EF colour line M, find the smallest whole-cent lift L such that `ceil(gap / (L × EF share)) ≤ M`. With the $12,000 gap, yellow at 36 needs $666.67; green at 12 needs $2,000. Debt uses the smallest lift whose method debt share, added to minimum payments, pays off within M months. Base surplus independently adds the full lift directly: `max(threshold − base surplus, 0)`; the method defaults are yellow $500 and green $1,000. No claim that the surplus share alone crosses that threshold. Entering a $2,000 lift displays EF $1,000, debt $500, surplus $500, EF timing 12 months (green), debt timing/colour and base surplus colour. Total income shown is approved recurring income plus the lift. Zero shares and missing facts do not create a reachable requirement.

3. **Debt amortization.** Only consumer debts, excluding mortgages. Known individual balances, APRs and minimums are required. Each month accrue `balance × APR/1200`, pay each minimum (capped at its balance), then allocate the extra and freed minimums in descending APR order. The aggregate minimum budget remains fixed. No fees or future rate changes are assumed. The loop caps at 1,200 months; a known debt beyond that cap is red with timing unavailable. Summary balances without rates/minimums and unknown individual inputs have unknown timing/colour. Confirmed zero debt takes zero months (green). At 12% APR, $1,000 balance and $100 monthly minimum with no lift: after ten payments $58.40 remains; month eleven clears it. At 0% APR, $6,000 balance, $500 minimum and a $1,000 lift (25% extra = $250) clear in eight months, green.

4. **Optionality.** Continuing income combines business, filler/continuing work and passive income from approved sources. Before the draw, gap = max(monthly need − continuing income, 0). The savings draw defaults to that gap and follows edits until explicitly overridden, including $0. Remaining business funding gap = max(need − continuing income − explicit draw, 0); annual required business pay = 12 × that gap. The runway depletes savings by the entered savings draw, rather than subtracting it twice. Each month `balance = max(balance × (1 + 0.05/12) − draw, 0)` and count the month, including the final partial month. At 1,200 months label indefinitely. Unknown income or balances remain unknown. $60,000 savings and $3,000 draw: $2,767.51 remains after month 20; month 21 exhausts savings, so approximately **21 months / 1yr 9mo**, yellow. Green is 36+, yellow 18–35, red below 18. When continuing sources cover need, say “All sources cover the need — business income is pure upside.” Returns remain uncertain.

5. **Priority statuses.** EF uses method target lines (defaults ≤12 green, ≤36 yellow, otherwise red). Debt uses the same lines with interest-aware timing, including green. Wealth uses an explicit household wealth colour only; the current source model has no such colour, so it remains unknown, never inferred green from net worth. Experiences + Life preserves the prototype's ≤24 EF-month yellow line, otherwise red; the method's default yellow line is 36 and would materially loosen this rule, so it is deliberately not substituted.

## Deliberate differences and retained fixes

The sample-household $800 EF/debt caps are removed. Program method shares replace fixed 50/25 constants. Multiple consumer debts use avalanche ordering and freed minimums. Unknowns stay unknown rather than assuming $0, APR, minimums or a wealth status. Computation preserves fractional cents in annual allocations; lift requirements round upward to the smallest whole cent. Experiences keeps 24 months. Optionality distinguishes the pre-draw gap, savings withdrawal and remaining business need so the withdrawal is not deducted twice.

All four bug fixes remain: debt may be green within the green line; bonuses/one-time income are excluded from base surplus; draw prefill is gap-derived and preserves explicit zero; Mia and tools share Rails facts with no scenario writes or missing-plan initialization. Program tool gates remain intact; persona YAML is unchanged.
