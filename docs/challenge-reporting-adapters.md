# Approved checkpoint reporting and daily reminder attendance

Sponsor reports now read participant-approved S07 checkpoint versions through `ChallengePrivacy::ApprovedCheckpointAdapter`. Report approval, read and CSV retain the fixed S08 policy: one immutable report per cohort/day/policy, only Day 30/60/90, minimum cells of five, complement suppression, and suppression when one through four participants change bands between scheduled reports. There are no individual amounts, identities, raw statements, reflections, filters or summed household savings in the public report.

The scheduled cutoff is the cohort start plus milestone day minus one. It is validated even when no participants currently consent to reporting. The first sealed report also pins the cohort calendar for later reports. A participant who starts later remains `late_window` at the scheduled cohort cutoff, even if their later personal checkpoint is eventually approved. A personal window preceding the cohort window fails closed.

The production adapter verifies the approved head's participant identity, version and approver, then validates the frozen snapshot against its original accepted release, personal calendar, approved financial/daily sequences, plan selection and baseline history. It fingerprints the immutable version identity and complete validated snapshot. Classification reads that snapshot rather than current balances or the current target. An unapproved correction leaves the approved version visible; a later approved correction changes a new adapter observation, but cannot rebuild an already sealed sponsor report.

Classification priority is withdrawal, late personal window, missing approved checkpoint, absent target, custom target, unknown reporting, pending Day 90 final confirmation, and then achieved/below the accepted $500 target. The full target applies at every milestone; no invented prorated target is introduced. Custom or absent targets can coexist with unknown savings and their bands do not establish a known financial result. Known progress at Day 90 remains `final_pending` until the participant explicitly approves final confirmation. Self-reported savings do not become bank-verified savings through reporting.

A missing, late or withdrawn checkpoint has private identity `unapproved:<enrollment id>:<milestone>` with a nil checkpoint version and a deterministic calendar/reason fingerprint. Only `unknown`, `late_window` and `withdrawn` admit this sentinel. It never represents an approved checkpoint version. Actual approved observations use positive checkpoint IDs and version numbers. Neither identity form nor the private observation digest appears in the public report or CSV.

Every fixed report read reauthorizes the staff actor, program enablement/hold/status, participant household and membership scope, current runtime, roster, consent version, and expiry. Revocation or a changed participation/runtime/calendar invalidates the report; it cannot be dynamically recalculated at the same checkpoint. The explicit adapter injection remains a trusted server-side test seam, not a client-selectable reporting source.

`ChallengeReminders::Attendance` is now the default for Eligibility, Scheduler and Delivery, including recurring Recovery. Suppression requires the participant's current approved `spending` or `no_spend` check-in for the exact local date. An unknown report, purchase draft, approved purchase without a check-in, reflection, chat, notification, or dismissal does not suppress reminders. Independently changed canonical purchases and a contradictory no-spend report also prevent suppression. A spending check-in can be complete without an entered purchase amount; completion does not attest complete bank/account coverage. Program holds, withdrawal and permission loss fail closed.

No outbound provider was invoked for validation. These adapters add no routes, migration, operation catalog changes or frontend workflow. Email remains disabled unless the separately documented operator configuration and participant consent are both present.

Validation on the isolated reporting branch passed the full backend suite (2,445 tests and 38,442 assertions), RuboCop, Brakeman, bundler audit and Zeitwerk. Focused tests exercise thirty synthetic participants with mixed checkpoint states, immutable replay, revocation, known zero, unknown reporting, final confirmation, forged identities and daily attendance boundaries. Separate PostgreSQL connections confirmed that concurrent sealing creates one report with one set of observations, and that consent revocation waits behind an authorized locked read before denying subsequent reads. All fixtures used a disposable test database; no real participants or provider sends were involved.
